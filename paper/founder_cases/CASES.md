# Founder firings: case studies

Rules: D-35 in `../DECISIONS.md` (fixed before any per-firm return was read). Cases: the coordinator's list `h1_list.csv`, 103 founder departures: the 77 founders in H1 (D-34) and flagged others, including the 55 well-dated founder firings of the main sample (D-35). Five coders, batches A to E; fields are merged as written, numbers are the pack's (`founder_cases_data.csv`). Each case's sources are in its batch file and in `founder_cases.csv`. A cell under 10 cases gets names, not shares.

Shareholder class (D-35, first rule that applies): bankrupt, taken over, delisted other (all within five years); otherwise the three-year buy-and-hold return against the matched firm: gained (> +10 points), lost (< -10), about even. The thresholds are the analyst's proposal.

## 7. Largest three-year dollar gaps against the matched firm (2023 dollars)

**Largest losses:** Altaba Inc (2008, other): -$38.4 billion (-128%); Enron Corp (2000, other): -$38.4 billion (-37%); Worldcom Inc (2002, fraud): -$27.4 billion (-70%); Legg Mason Inc (2008, other): -$16.3 billion (-114%); Wynn Resorts Ltd (2018, scandal): -$9.2 billion (-43%).

**Largest gains:** Chipotle Mexican Grill Inc (2017, other): +$36.7 billion (+323%); Ralph Lauren Corp (2015, other): +$10.4 billion (+80%); Broadcom Corp -Cl A (2003, other): +$10.2 billion (+130%); Aes Corp (2002, other): +$9.4 billion (+126%); World Wrestling Entmt Inc (2022, scandal): +$7.2 billion (+139%).

Sum across the 91 cases with a three-year matched return: -$70.0 billion; without Enron: -$31.6 billion. Descriptive only; H1's tests are in the paper (D-34).

## 8. Notable facts, as recorded by the coders

- Wisconsin Central Transportn (1998): His later railway, Montreal Maine & Atlantic, ran the train in the 2013 Lac-Megantic disaster (outside the 5 years).
- Just For Feet Inc (1999): The company filed for Chapter 11 within months of his exit (bankrupt in the data within 1 year); the accounting fraud surfaced later.
- Sunrise Medical Inc (1999): Taken over within five years (pack delisting code: acquired). The founder had built it from $50,000 to $660 million in sales in 15 years (LA Times).
- Trueblue Inc (2000): Founded a rival day-labour staffing company, Command Center, within about three years.
- Phycor Inc (2000): Shares rose 11.8% on the news; the firm filed Chapter 11 in 2002 anyway.
- Egghead.Com Inc (2000): Kaplan founded GO Corp, an early tablet-computer maker, before Onsale (jerrykaplan.com via database notes).
- Ca Inc (2000): His successor Sanjay Kumar was later sentenced to 12 years in prison for the accounting fraud at CA (Wikipedia, Computer Associates). The firing is coded as performance; the revenue fraud surfaced after it.
- Pss World Medical Inc (2000): The database note records that his management advice book shows him on a skateboard (unverified).
- Lechters Inc (2000): Bankrupt in April 2001, about five months after the new CEO arrived (bankruptcy date from the pack).
- Enron Corp (2000): Enron filed for bankruptcy on 2 December 2001, under a year after the handover (Wikipedia). This one case is about -$40 billion against the matched firm and -$88 billion against the S&P 500 over three years, in 2023 dollars.
- Take-Two Interactive Sftwr (2001): First CEO convicted over option backdating (Feb 2007), six years after leaving the CEO job.
- Provell Inc -Cl A (2001): One insider profile lists Cohn as CEO until July 2002, conflicting with the 2001 exit.
- Cree Inc (2001): He stayed chairman for four more years.
- Internap Corp (2001): In 2006 he publicly called Internap's VitalStream purchase 'disappointing' (Light Reading headline).
- Edgewater Technology Inc (2002): A shareholder said: "Clete had the skills to ride a lawn mower, and his daddy gave him a Ferrari" (database note).
- Mayors Jewelers Inc/De (2002): The firm was taken over within five years; shareholders beat the matched firm by 66 points over three years.
- Worldcom Inc (2002): WorldCom filed for Chapter 11 on 21 July 2002, under three months after the board removed him; it was then the largest US bankruptcy (Wikipedia, WorldCom). Fraud cell, kept out of the main sample.
- Valeant Pharmaceuticals -Old (2002): He had been Prime Minister of Yugoslavia in 1992-93 while running ICN, and ran against Milosevic for the Serbian presidency (Wikipedia). ICN renamed itself Valeant in 2003.
- Aes Corp (2002): Shares nearly tripled against the market in the three years after he left.
- Aspen Technology Inc (2002): The database note says Evans pleaded guilty; the sources found say the guilty plea was McQuillin's and Evans settled a civil case. The database's legal code (4) reflects the later case, not why he left.
- Broadcom Corp -Cl A (2003): Charges brought five years later were thrown out by a judge who called the prosecution's witness intimidation 'shameful'.
- Impath Inc (2003): Fraud-coded, outside the main sample. The data show no delisting within 5 years although IMPATH is described as defunct; check the delisting record.
- Encompass Health Corp (2003): Acquitted of the HealthSouth fraud, yet a civil judge later called him the 'CEO of the fraud' (WP:Richard_M._Scrushy).
- Astropower Inc (2003): The SEC case arrived six years after the firm had gone.
- Viavi Solutions Inc (2003): By market value the largest founder exit in this batch (about $9.3bn); its dollar figures dominate founder totals.
- Axt Inc (2004): A boomerang: the board that removed him in 2004 brought him back as CEO in 2009.
- Neomagic Corp (2005): Severance agreement signed five weeks after a 'personal reasons' exit.
- Swift Transportation Co Inc (2005): Boomerang: took Swift private at $31.55 a share in 2007 and returned as CEO; re-listed it in 2010.
- Calpine Corp (2005): Bankruptcy came 22 days after the ouster; the shares fell 58.7% over the three days.
- Pharmanet Development Group (2006): Exit announced the same day the chair also resigned.
- Lca Vision Inc (2006): After leaving, the founder bought into his old firm's largest competitor (Bloomberg, 2 Jul 2006: "When Dad Bets on an Archrival").
- Inter-Tel Inc -Ser A (2006): The fired founder ran a proxy fight against his own board within three months and then bid for the company; it was taken over within five years.
- Biomet Inc (2006): Biomet was bought by a private-equity consortium (Blackstone, Goldman Sachs, KKR, TPG) in a September 2007 club deal, 18 months after the founder left (Wikipedia, Biomet).
- Vertro Inc (2006): Both founders left on the same day.
- Sun Microsystems Inc (2006): Oracle agreed to buy Sun in April 2009, three years after the handover; the EU approved the deal in January 2010 (Wikipedia, Sun Microsystems).
- Carmax Inc (2006): The push was public only six years later, when Fortune reported the managers' ultimatum.
- Comverse Technology Inc (2006): A fugitive founder: he fled to Namibia within months of the firing. Comverse was delisted from Nasdaq and removed from the S&P 500 while it restated years of accounts (Wikipedia), so there is no clean three-year return.
- Atmel Corp (2006): The fired founder tried to fire the board back through a special shareholder meeting in 2007.
- Career Education Corp (2006): Left six weeks after a 29% one-day share fall.
- Monster Worldwide Inc (2006): Died about two years after the firing, months after settling with prosecutors and the SEC.
- Sapient Corp (2006): Fraud-coded, outside the main sample.
- Jetblue Airways Corp (2007): His next airline, Azul, carried 2.2 million passengers in its first year, beating JetBlue's start-up record (CNN Money/Fortune 2010).
- Clearfield Inc (2007): Market value $22m six weeks before; the stock beat its competitor by 416 points over five years (h1 pr_m60).
- Mannatech Inc (2007): Banned from working for his company for five years, yet stayed on its board.
- Legg Mason Inc (2008): Wikipedia dates his retirement to 2007 ("He retired in 2007 after 46 years as chairman, president, and CEO"), while the hand-checked day 0 is 28 Jan 2008. Legg Mason was sold to Franklin Templeton in 2020.
- Presidential Life Corp (2008): Dated 13 months before the actual handover (D-30 flags this for the second coder).
- Applied Signal Technology (2008): Shares jumped 23.6% on the news; Raytheon agreed to buy the firm for $38 a share in Dec 2010.
- Alpha Natural Resources Inc (2008): Founder stayed chairman about three and a half years after the handover (unverified, source title only).
- Sunrise Senior Living Inc (2008): Sunrise was sold to Health Care REIT for about $845m in 2012-13, after near bankruptcy in 2009.
- Altaba Inc (2008): Shares rose 9.7% over the three days around the news, among the largest gains in this batch.
- Datascope Corp (2009): An activist (Ramius) attacked the founder over ethics reports in November 2007; just over a year later the company was sold and he left with it. The buyer is not named in the filings read.
- Progress Software Corp (2009): Shares rose 8.5% on the three days of the news.
- Jazz Pharmaceuticals Plc (2009): Market value only $34m six weeks before; five years later the stock had beaten its competitor by about 10,350 points (h1 pr_m60 = 103.5), the largest gain in the founder sample.
- Ruby Tuesday Inc (2012): The company was taken private by NRD Capital in a $146 million deal announced in October 2017, just after the five-year window (Wikipedia, Ruby Tuesday).
- Digital River Inc (2012): Left both the CEO job and the board within two months.
- Chesapeake Energy Corp (2013): Started a rival oil company within three days of leaving and died the day after his indictment.
- True Religion Apparel Inc (2013): The company was taken over within five years (h1/db: delisted, acquired); buyer and price not read.
- Amedisys Inc (2014): Fraud-coded, outside the main sample (comparison group).
- Dolan Co (2014): Founder of a firm he formed in 1992 with Cherry Tree Investments; described as 'a protégé of publishing magnate Rupert Murdoch' (MinnPost).
- Tile Shop Holdings Inc (2014): A boomerang: back as CEO three years after stepping down, for no salary. Shareholders beat the matched firm by 104 points over three years.
- Five Below Inc (2014): The successor ran the firm for almost ten years; sales went from $1.0bn in 2016 to $3.08bn in 2022 (WP:Five_Below).
- Sagent Pharmaceuticals Inc (2015): Taken over within five years (db: acquired); buyer and price not read.
- Guess Inc (2015): The reason in the database belongs to his 2018 exit as chairman, not the 2015 handover.
- Iconix Brand Group Inc (2015): First jury acquitted Cole on two counts; a retrial convicted him on all eight.
- Ralph Lauren Corp (2015): The founder outlasted his hand-picked outside successor, who left within two years.
- First Nbc Bank Holding Co (2016): The bank's holding company went bankrupt in April 2017 (pack date), 16 months after the demotion; the shares had already fallen about 90% (nola.com).
- Northern Oil & Gas Inc (2016): Fired in 2016, paid off in 2017, rehired as CEO in 2018.
- Bankunited Inc (2016): Exit took effect four months after the announcement (2017-01-01, DB).
- Siriuspoint Ltd (2017): none beyond the date gap: the transition was announced on 23 Feb 2017, six days before the date used.
- Edgewater Technology Inc (2017): Activist won the board by written consent in February, and the new board fired the founder three weeks later.
- Synchronoss Technologies (2017): A founder who came back as CEO and was out again within about seven months (DB).
- Chipotle Mexican Grill Inc (2017): Largest gain in the batch among large firms: three-year return 323 points above the matched firm (db mbhar_3y), 337 points above the prior-return competitor (h1 pr_m36).
- Papa Johns International Inc (2017): He had already left and returned once before: stepped down in 2005, back in 2008.
- Rent-A-Center Inc (2017): A founder boomerang that failed: Speese came back as CEO in 2017 and was out again within nine months.
- Diplomat Pharmacy Inc (2018): Agreed to a UnitedHealth/Optum buyout in December 2019 reported at about $300 million.
- Wynn Resorts Ltd (2018): Shares rose 9.6% over the three days of his resignation (h1 data via the paper), yet lost 43 points against the matched firm over three years.
- Sonic Automotive Inc -Cl A (2018): Founder family kept control: one brother replaced another.
- Apollo Medical Holding Inc (2019): The company later renamed itself Astrana Health (name in h1 list ticker ASTH).
- Approach Resources Inc (2019): Bankrupt within the year (db delist_kind_5y = bankruptcy).
- Laredo Petroleum Inc (2019): The firm later renamed itself Vital Energy (WP:Vital_Energy).
- Green Dot Corp (2019): Shares fell 10.4% on the news day (LA Times).
- Klx Energy Servs Hldng (2020): Founder status is arguable: Khoury founded the parent KLX Inc. and B/E Aerospace; KLXE was spun off in 2018. The database note says his son was indicted in the college-admissions case five months later (unverified).
- Smart Global Holdings Inc (2020): Shah had himself returned to SMART as CEO in 2018 after serving as executive chairman.
- Viasat Inc (2020): The founder handed over the CEO job and took it back 20 months later.
- Adapthealth Corp (2021): The 'fraud' was personal, not the firm's: by the paper's own definitions this is closer to a personal-conduct firing.
- Enzo Biochem Inc (2021): The shares fell 19.5% over the three days of the news, one of the largest falls among the founders.
- Greenbrier Companies Inc (2021): Tekorius was only the second CEO in the company's history.
- World Wrestling Entmt Inc (2022): Came back as executive chair six months after his exit, using his majority vote, and sold the company within a year.
- Eagle Pharmaceuticals Inc (2023): The shares fell 29.6% over the three days, the largest announcement fall in batch A. Too recent for a three-year return.
